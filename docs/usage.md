# Use the library

The graph is the home workspace. Its search bar gives you three ways to work
without making you leave the library:

| Mode | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Ask** | A question whose details may appear across several sources. |
| **Records** | Finding library entries by their text or names. |
| **Commands** | Finding an action in the interface. |

## Ask a question

Write a normal question and name its subject: **“When is the Lantern pilot's
weekly review?”** is more useful than **“When is it?”** You do not need a dense
paragraph of keywords. Ask another question when you want to explore a different
detail.

Additional questions are optional. They request more details about the same
subject; they are not filters that every source must satisfy. The advanced
controls remain available when you need more precision.

Quotes open alongside the graph. Close the results to return to the same graph
position, or reopen them with **View quotes**.

## Read a result

Each quotation belongs to a particular source. The short excerpt emphasizes
text matching your question; expand it to read the full returned passage. Click
the source title or location to open the source reader. Original PDF page and
region images require the optional `document-view` dependency.

Dates and document versions matter. If two passages disagree, inspect their
versions and context before choosing which one supports your answer. A saved
memory may also have correction history.

Related files and connected records are routes to explore when a matching
quotation is unavailable. Open them to check their contents. They are not extra
quotations or independent support for a claim.

## Copy or keep what you find

The copy action exports the loaded source passages and saved memories with
citations. Copying a selection restricts it to the selected sources among those
results. It does not include file-title matches, connection hints or generated
annotations as if they were source quotations. It copies the full returned
passages, rather than only the visually emphasized sentence.

You can copy without saving anything. To resume an investigation later, select
sources and save a **collection**. Collections keep references, your notes,
review decisions and unresolved questions together. Open them through
**Library → Collections**.

A **writing example** is a passage kept as a style or tone reference for an
assistant. It is separate from evidence supporting a factual statement and has
nothing to do with microphone audio.

## Explore and customize

Use Graph to follow recorded relationships, or use Library to browse memories,
files, collections and imports. Review handles records needing attention;
Health and Connection expose library status and connectivity. Graph controls,
groups, themes and motion preferences remain available alongside Ask.

## If you cannot find something

Load more when offered, try another question, or inspect the file directly.
Search has delivery and extraction limits, so an empty result does not establish
that the information is absent. Result details expose diagnostics when you need
to investigate a particular search. See the [FAQ](faq.md) for the distinctions
between source text, discovery hints and unsupported claims.
